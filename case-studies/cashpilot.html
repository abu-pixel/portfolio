CashPilot

A business ledger where a credit sale is one action: the sale is recorded, the customer's debt is updated and stock is adjusted together, or not at all.

01Project overview

CashPilot is a native Android business management app that connects cash flow, inventory and customer debt in one ledger. It is built with Kotlin, Jetpack Compose and Room. The defining behaviour is a credit sale: one user action writes the sale, the amount owed by the customer and the stock change as a single database transaction.

02The real problem

Bookkeeping apps usually treat financial records, inventory and customer debt as separate things. That splits one real event into three manual entries, and if one is missed the owner's view of the business is wrong. Reconciling the difference later is the kind of work a small business owner does after hours, not during the sale.

03Target users and their needs

  • Small business owners using Android who sell goods, sometimes on credit.
  • They need to know what is in stock, what they are owed and what came in — from the same screen.
  • They need the app to work locally on the device, without depending on a server I would have to run.
  • They are not accounting specialists; the ledger should reflect what actually happened.

04System architecture

05Technology selection and justification

  • Kotlin and Jetpack Compose give a native Android experience with less UI boilerplate than XML views.
  • Room is a relational store with real transaction support, which is what the credit-sale invariant needs.
  • MVVM with a repository keeps Compose screens free of data-access logic and makes the write path testable in principle.
  • Kotlin Flows let the UI react to ledger changes without manually refreshing each screen.
  • Material 3 supplies familiar components and accessibility behaviour without custom widgets.

06Database schema / data model

Three sets of records are linked: sales or transactions that describe money coming in, customers with an outstanding debt balance, and inventory items with a quantity on hand. A credit sale touches all three. Reports read from those records rather than keeping a separate copy of the truth.

Sales

Amount, date and link to the customer and items involved.

Customers

Identity plus the running amount owed to the business.

Inventory

Items and quantities adjusted when a sale is recorded.

Consistency

All three writes share one Room transaction boundary.

This is a conceptual description. I have not published a schema diagram or table listing, so the Room entities and DAOs in the repository remain the exact definition.

07API documentation

There is no HTTP API in this project. The internal surface is the repository interface and the Room DAOs that back it. I have not published a DAO reference, so I am not inventing endpoint names here; the repository is the documentation.

08Authentication and authorization

Not applicable in this version. No account system, login or multi-user permission model is part of the described stack; records live in the app's local storage on the device. I am stating that plainly rather than implying an auth layer the app does not have.

14Security considerations

CashPilot keeps its records locally in the app's storage and does not list a network API in its stack, so there is no remote service to expose business data. I would not describe it as an encrypted password-style vault: no encryption feature is listed for this project. Android's app sandbox is the main protection, and device-level security is the user's responsibility.

15Difficult technical challenges

The hard part was consistency, not any single screen. A credit sale changes three things, and a failure halfway through would leave the books wrong. Putting the writes inside one Room transaction makes the operation all-or-nothing. Wiring that transaction through a repository rather than directly into a Compose handler kept the UI from owning data-integrity rules.

16Engineering trade-offs

  • Local-only storage: simple and private, in exchange for no sync across devices or off-device backup in the described scope.
  • Room transactions: strict consistency for related writes, in exchange for thinking carefully about what belongs in one boundary.
  • Native Android: a better platform fit, in exchange for no iOS or web client from the same code.
  • MVVM and Flows: clearer separation and reactive UI, in exchange for more classes than a single-activity approach.

17Known limitations

  • Android only, with no live web demo or public download linked here.
  • No login, multi-user support or permission model.
  • No described sync, backup or export flow beyond local storage.
  • No published test report or performance measurements.

19Outcomes and measurable results

The concrete result I can point to is the atomic credit-sale flow: one action updates the sale, the debt and the stock without leaving the records out of step. I have not measured time saved, retention or any business result, so none is claimed here.
