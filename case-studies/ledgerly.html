01Project overview

Ledgerly, originally called Ledger Intelligence, is a mobile bookkeeping and cash-flow app for small business owners. It records income and expenses, creates invoices, turns the ledger into cash-flow reports and offers an AI bookkeeping assistant. I built it as a personal project to learn how a full-stack product holds together from the database to the phone screen.

02The real problem

Small business owners in my area often keep income and expenses in notebooks or spreadsheets. Reconciling them costs time, and the result is usually a balance rather than a clear picture of where money is going. The tools I looked at assumed an account, a sync setup and a bigger budget than the people I had in mind.

03Target users and their needs

  • Owner-operators and very small teams who handle their own books on a phone.
  • They need to log a transaction in seconds, even with a receipt attached.
  • They need invoices they can send, and reports that answer "where did the money go?"
  • They are not asking for a full ERP; they need the ledger to be correct first.

04System architecture

05Technology selection and justification

  • Expo and React Native let one TypeScript codebase serve the mobile apps and a web target, faster than maintaining two native clients.
  • TypeScript on both sides keeps client and server shapes aligned as the ledger grows.
  • Express gives a small surface for the server-side AI proxy, which is the simplest way to keep a key off the device.
  • PostgreSQL with Drizzle ORM suits relational records and cash-flow aggregation, with typed queries instead of raw string SQL.
  • Clerk avoids writing session handling, password reset and account screens myself.
  • Pollinations AI was usable for experimentation without a paid key; it is called only through the server.

06Database schema / data model

The domain groups into three parts: identity supplied by Clerk, ledger records covering income and expense transactions with optional receipt references, and invoices that point to a customer and carry line items. Reports are derived from the transaction set rather than stored as a second source of truth.

Identity

Users are managed by Clerk; the database stores records belonging to a signed-in identity.

Ledger

Transactions carry amount, direction and date; receipts are optional attachments.

Invoices

Invoices reference a customer and line items, and relate back to the ledger.

Reports

Cash-flow views are computed from transactions, not persisted as authoritative data.

I have not published a formal schema document. The migrations in the repository are the exact source of truth, and this section describes the model conceptually on purpose.

07API documentation

The Express server exposes resources for ledger transactions, invoices, cash-flow reports and the AI assistant. Requests that touch ledger data are made with a Clerk session. I have not published a full endpoint reference yet, so I am not listing routes here; the route definitions in the repository are the current documentation.

08Authentication and authorization

Clerk handles authentication. The app signs users in, and the server verifies the session before reading or writing ledger records. There is no separate role model in this version: a signed-in user works with their own records. Multi-user books and granular permissions are not implemented, and I have not added them to avoid implying a capability the app does not have.

14Security considerations

  • The AI provider key lives on the Express server and is never shipped in the mobile bundle.
  • Only aggregate figures are sent to the AI assistant — no client names and no receipt images.
  • Clerk session tokens are verified server-side before ledger data is accessed.
  • Traffic between client and API uses HTTPS; no credentials are stored in local app code.

15Difficult technical challenges

Keeping the AI key off the device meant proxying every assistant request through Express and deciding exactly what could leave the app. That constraint shaped the feature: the assistant reasons over aggregate numbers, never individual receipts or customer names. Structuring the monorepo so web and mobile share logic without sharing secrets was the other challenge, and it is what pushed the configuration into the server package.

16Engineering trade-offs

  • Free AI API: no cost to experiment, but availability and rate limits are outside my control.
  • Managed auth: less security code to maintain, in exchange for a third-party dependency.
  • Server proxy: one extra network hop, in exchange for keeping the key off the phone.
  • Postgres plus Drizzle: more setup than a document store, in exchange for typed queries and real aggregation.

17Known limitations

  • Depends on a free third-party AI API whose limits I do not control.
  • No offline capture and later sync; the app expects a connection.
  • No multi-user books, roles or granular permissions.
  • No published schema document, API reference or performance/test report.
  • Accounts are managed by Clerk, so using the app requires an account even though viewing this case study does not.

18Future development roadmap

These are ideas I would explore, not commitments or funded work:

  • Publish the schema and a short endpoint reference alongside the repository.
  • Add an automated test suite for the ledger and invoice flows.
  • Support offline transaction capture with reconciliation on reconnect.
  • Add shared books with explicit permissions and an audit trail.

19Outcomes and measurable results

The live demo is reachable. The outcome I can describe precisely is a working end-to-end flow: sign in, log a transaction, raise an invoice, read a cash-flow report and ask an assistant a question without sending a receipt or a customer name anywhere. I have not measured business impact, response times or conversion, so I am not claiming any.

20My contribution

I designed and built Ledgerly as a personal project: the Expo client, the Express API, the data model, the privacy decisions around the AI assistant and deployment. There was no team to credit and no client brief, and I would rather keep that clear than dress it up as production work.
